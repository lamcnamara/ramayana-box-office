# Decisions

## Reporting rules adopted 22 November 2026

| Question | Working decision |
| --- | --- |
| Which day can lead the report? | Publish the last contiguous day with complete-day coverage for all three markets. A clearly labeled full-day estimate can qualify. Keep a partial ticket count, missing territory, conflicting definition or live day in the dated review with a specific next check. |
| Which figure goes in which column? | Use The Numbers’ domestic daily USD for US/Canada and cross-check with Box Office Mojo. Use Sacnilk’s all-language India **gross** and **worldwide** day-wise estimates in ₹ crore. Worldwide already includes India and overseas. A dated distributor statement with matching scope is evidence for resolving a conflict. Record its original URL and the decision. |
| How are revisions handled? | Prefer the current dated table when its daily rows reconcile to the publisher’s dated cumulative. News copies and cached snippets can preserve an earlier revision. Record the old and new value for each changed date and metric. An unresolved disagreement goes in the review with both links. |
| How are previews and weeks treated? | Allocate Boxoffy’s rounded $2,000,000 to 4 November and subtract it from the $3,505,682 opening-day report for 5 November. Together the rows equal the reported opening. India and worldwide previews have no separate entry. The first full-week comparison includes the preview once and uses Thursday–Wednesday weeks. Weekend comparisons use Friday–Sunday. |
| How is currency handled? | Preserve the ₹94/USD baseline so editions remain comparable. FRED’s H.10 series is a reference for a possible dated-rate view. Keep the observation date, rate and weekend convention if that feature is developed. |
| What about the narrative and ratings? | Keep the first-16-days essay anchored to 20 November. Recheck its wording after historical corrections. Reception figures are a separately dated observation and move only after checking their cited source. |
| What changed in the first review? | R-001 records the five revised worldwide daily values. Their sum through 20 November agrees with the Sacnilk day-16 report. The 21 November North American amount remains open. |

## Regional reception, 22 November 2026

The [reception section](../../ramayana-box-office-snapshot-v2.html#reception) pairs audience evidence with signed criticism for India and the US/Canada market. Locations refer to the screenings, outlets or platforms. PTI’s opening-day interviews and the Los Angeles premiere account give individual reactions. BookMyShow reports voluntary votes, PostTrak reports opening-weekend exit polling, and Rotten Tomatoes publishes sitewide scores. Preserve these distinctions when revising the copy. The reception date is independent of the box-office cutoff.

## International edition · R-003, 22 November 2026

Use the local chart’s market boundary, currency, reporting window and precision. The six recurring markets are the UK and Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, the Netherlands, Portugal and Nigeria. Nigeria follows Fusion Intelligence’s trade series. FDA/Comscore’s second UK/Ireland weekend differs by £1 from the BFI/UK Cinema Association figure. Retain both attributions. The BFI opening includes £435,875 in previews, which the weekend comparison removes once.

A weekend, a full week, a chart date and an inspection date describe different things. Keep admission counts in tickets, local money in its published currency and Mojo’s USD reports in USD. Keep Spain’s two catalogue records separate and show their combined receipts only when their reporting basis matches. Release-only entries have no amount. The worldwide series already includes overseas revenue. These selected reports cannot reconstruct the worldwide residual.

Regional reception identifies the critic, publication, ticketing platform or community being described. In Brazil, the review refers to the long Hindi version. Vietnamese MoMo ratings describe participating ticket buyers, and the Australian commentary is an interpretation of audience composition.
