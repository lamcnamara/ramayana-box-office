Ramayana: Part 1 (2026)

Dir. Nitesh Tiwari

Converts India and worldwide grosses from rupees. North America is reported in US dollars.

₹94 per US dollar

In the Ramayana, Prince Rama accepts exile to honour his father’s word, then sets out to rescue his wife Sita from Ravana, king of Lanka. Tradition credits this Hindu epic to the poet Valmiki. Its Sanskrit text grew over centuries from around the fifth century BCE. Its questions about duty live on in North India’s Ramlila plays, drawn from Tulsidas’s 16th-century Awadhi retelling, and in Thailand’s masked khon dance.1

Tiwari’s film opens a two-part adaptation that set out to bring the epic to IMAX audiences worldwide. DNEG, a visual effects and animation studio working across film, television and immersive experiences, helped create its kingdoms and battles.2

The First Two Weeks

5–18 November 2026 · Worldwide theatrical release

In its first 14 days in cinemas, Ramayana grossed an estimated ₹1,519.05 crore worldwide. India contributed ₹1,009.05 crore, or 66.4% of the worldwide gross. US and Canadian theaters earned $24.11M during the same 14 days, excluding advance previews.

Worldwide ticket sales fell from ₹944.55 crore in the first seven days to ₹574.50 crore in the next seven, a 39.2% decline.

Critical and audience reception

Reviewed 22 November 2026

India

Audiences

Viewers who chose to rate the film on BookMyShow gave it 9.2/10 from 472,000+ votes. On opening day, one moviegoer told the Press Trust of India that Yash’s understated performance matched the Ravana they had always imagined. Another singled out his Sanskrit delivery. A less impressed viewer preferred the performances in the television serial and found the film’s dialogue disappointing.

BookMyShow viewer ratings · PTI opening-day interviews

Critics

In The Indian Express, Shubhra Gupta wrote that the film won her over by the halfway point. She found room for familiar family melodrama even amid the magic and battles. Aishani Biswas of Outlook admired Yash’s Ravana but missed the bond between Rama and his brother Bharata. For her, Bharata’s refusal to take the throne makes the cost of Rama’s exile felt at home.

The Indian Express · Outlook

North America (US and Canada)

Audiences

In PostTrak’s opening-weekend exit poll, 86% of respondents gave the film one of the two highest ratings, and 75% said they would definitely recommend it. At the earlier, invitation-only Los Angeles premiere, one viewer recalled seeing The Fellowship of the Ring for the first time and described calling out in protest when the film reached its cliffhanger.

Weekend report on PostTrak · Los Angeles premiere account

Critics

Writing in Canada’s Exclaim!, Alisha Mughal traced Ravana’s anger into the landscape itself as the rock around him melts. In the US, William Bibbiani of TheWrap came to the epic as a newcomer and delighted in the giant bird attacking a flying lotus. Simon Abrams at RogerEbert.com found the digital scenes stiff and struggled to feel much for the people in them.

Exclaim! · TheWrap · RogerEbert.com

Criticism

United Kingdom

Peter Bradshaw found Rama’s exile unexpectedly lively. In The Guardian, he singled out the sunset landscapes and the eccentric flying machine as pleasures of seeing the story on this scale.

Australia

In Indian Link, Torrsha Sen traced the opening to Indian-Australian communities. She described how viewers who knew the epic from family storytelling could encounter it again on an IMAX screen. The article gives a community perspective. It does not provide a film-specific demographic survey.

Criticism and viewer reviews

France

Isabelle Arnaud admired the costumes and score but found the digital treatment of some actors distracting. On AlloCiné, one viewer singled out Yash’s Ravana and called the storytelling dense. Another felt the music carried the long running time. Both chose to post their reviews.

Ticket buyers and criticism

Vietnam

MoMo displayed 9.3/10 from about 1,500 ratings by people who bought tickets on its platform. In VOH’s review, Ravana’s conflicting inner voices made him the more interesting character. The reviewer felt that the effects sometimes obscured the bond between Rama and Lakshman.

MoMo · ticket-buyer ratings · VOH · film review

Criticism

Brazil

Nane Tasca reviewed the long Hindi version for MeUGamer ahead of the Brazilian release. Seeing Ravana’s people suffer made his later cruelty intelligible to her, and she found the shifts between music, action and story kept the three hours moving. Her review identifies a shorter dubbed theatrical version.

MeUGamer · Nane Tasca
