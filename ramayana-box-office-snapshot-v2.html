Ramayana: Part 1 (2026)

Dir. Nitesh Tiwari

Converts India and worldwide grosses from rupees. North America is reported in US dollars.

₹94 per US dollar

In the Ramayana, Prince Rama accepts exile to honour his father’s word, then sets out to rescue his wife Sita from Ravana, king of Lanka. Tradition credits this Hindu epic to the poet Valmiki. Its Sanskrit text grew over centuries from around the fifth century BCE. Its questions about duty live on in North India’s Ramlila plays, drawn from Tulsidas’s 16th-century Awadhi retelling, and in Thailand’s masked khon dance.1

Tiwari’s film opens a two-part adaptation that set out to bring the epic to IMAX audiences worldwide. DNEG, a visual effects and animation studio working across film, television and immersive experiences, helped create its kingdoms and battles.2

The First Two Weeks

5–18 November 2026 · Worldwide theatrical release

In its first 14 days in cinemas, Ramayana grossed an estimated ₹1,519.05 crore worldwide. India contributed ₹1,009.05 crore, or 66.4% of the worldwide gross. US and Canadian theaters earned $24.11M during the same 14 days, excluding advance previews.

Worldwide ticket sales fell from ₹944.55 crore in the first seven days to ₹574.50 crore in the next seven, a 39.2% decline.
